# Step 6: The Cost of Status Quo

**Principle:** Name the believable, specific trajectory of getting worse if they don't act. Stakes create urgency.

## The rules

1. **Specific, not vague.** "They'll fall behind" is wrong. "They'll spend the next two quarters firefighting the same broken pipeline instead of shipping the new product" is right.
2. **Believable, not catastrophic.** "Their company will die" is hyperbole. The slow, real version is scarier.
3. **A trajectory, not a single event.** What gets *worse* over time. What compounds. What erodes.
4. **Two to four concrete losses.** Not one. Not ten. Two to four believable, specific things the user is allowed to lose.
5. **From their seat, not the company's.** What does *the user* personally lose: their time, their reputation, their leverage, their weekends?

## Opening probe

> What does it cost them to keep doing it the old way? Specifics: time, money, talent, opportunity, reputation.

## Pushback probes (yes-and pattern)

If the answer is generic ("they'll fall behind," "they'll lose money," "they'll be less competitive"):
> That's directionally right. Let's sharpen it. Behind on what, specifically? What's the version they'd recognize themselves in?

If the answer is catastrophic or fear-based ("their company will die," "they'll get fired"):
> That's one extreme. The slow, believable version is scarier and lands harder. What's the trajectory of getting worse, not the disaster ending?

If the answer is about the company instead of the user ("the company will lose revenue"):
> The company-level cost is real. From the user's seat, what do *they* lose personally? Time, reputation, leverage, weekends?

If the answer is a single line:
> That's the first cost. Two or three more compound on top of it. What else gets worse if they keep doing it the old way?

If the answer is positive in disguise ("they'll keep doing things the slower way"):
> "Slower" is the neutral version. What does slower actually cost? Missed quarters? More headcount needed? Senior people doing junior work?

## What passing looks like

Two to four concrete, believable losses — most of them from the user's personal seat. A trajectory that gets visibly worse over time without being catastrophic.

Examples of the *shape* (do not show these to the user):
- "Slower releases. More on-call pages. Senior engineers spending their best hours debugging instead of shipping. Eventually, a full migration under duress at the worst possible moment."
- "More LLM regressions caught by customers instead of dashboards. The team loses confidence in its own shipping cadence. The product loses ground to competitors who measure."

When the answer passes, mirror it back, confirm, and move to Step 7.
