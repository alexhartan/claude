# Step 2b: The Internal Friction

**Principle:** Name the emotional weight of the external problem. How does it feel to be the person living with it?

## The rules

1. **A feeling, not a fact.** "Their pipelines break" is external. "They feel like they're babysitting infrastructure instead of building" is internal.
2. **Specific feelings, not generic ones.** "Frustrated" isn't enough. Frustrated *about what, in front of whom, while trying to do what*.
3. **About the problem, not the solution.** "They're excited about our product" is wrong. We're naming what they feel *before* they ever hear of you.
4. **Honest.** Founders often soften this layer. Don't let them. The unvarnished feeling is what makes the copy land.

## Opening probe

> How does that external problem make them feel?

## Pushback probes (yes-and pattern)

If the answer is a single generic word ("frustrated," "annoyed," "stressed"):
> "[Their word]" is in the right zone. Let's go one layer deeper. Frustrated about what, specifically? Exposed in front of whom?

If the answer is product-feelings ("they want our product"):
> That's how they feel about the solution. The step is about how they feel about the problem, before they ever heard of you.

If the answer is softened or corporate ("they're looking for better solutions"):
> The polite version is there. Now the honest one. What do they actually feel at 11pm when the thing breaks again?

If the answer is too positive ("they're optimistic about the future"):
> Optimism is a useful frame, but not the friction. What's the version of this that keeps them up at night?

If the user resists ("I don't know what they feel"):
> Fair. Best guess based on what they've told you. What's the recurring complaint, the sigh, the thing they keep saying?

## What passing looks like

A single sentence naming a specific emotional state tied to the external friction.

Examples of the *shape* (do not show these to the user):
- "They feel like they're babysitting infrastructure instead of building, and they're starting to wonder if they're falling behind."
- "They feel exposed. Shipping LLM changes on instinct, hoping nothing regresses, knowing they can't actually prove it."
- "They feel embarrassed sending prospects to their own website. The product is the proudest thing they've built, and the site is the part they wish people wouldn't see."

When the answer passes, mirror it back, confirm, and move to Step 2c.
