# Step 1: The User

**Principle:** The user is the hero. Name them specifically enough that a stranger could picture them.

## The rules

1. **A person, not a category.** "Startups" is wrong. "Data engineers at growth-stage startups" is closer. "Heads of Data at Seed/Series A companies who own the data platform" is right.
2. **Role + context + stakes.** Their job title, the kind of company they work at, and what's on their plate this quarter.
3. **Singular.** One user. If they serve three personas, pick the front door — the one they want the most of.
4. **In the user's world, not in marketing world.** No "decision makers," no "stakeholders," no "buyers." Use the language the user would use about themselves.

## Opening probe

> Who is the hero of your product's story? Be specific. Role, what kind of company, what they care about.

## Pushback probes (yes-and pattern)

If the answer is a category ("startups," "businesses," "developers," "teams"):
> [Category] is the right shape. Let's narrow it. What's their actual role, and what kind of company are they at?

If the answer is a title with no context ("data engineers," "marketers"):
> Got the role. Now the context. What kind of company, what stage, what's on their plate this quarter?

If the answer names multiple personas:
> All three are real. Let's pick one for the front door. Which user do you want the most of, right now?

If the answer uses marketing language ("decision-makers," "buyers," "stakeholders"):
> That's the sales-team version. How would they describe themselves at a dinner party?

If the user resists narrowing:
> Fair instinct. The catch: every line of copy on the site is aimed at someone. If we don't pick, the site picks for you, and it usually picks wrong.

## What passing looks like

A single sentence naming the user with enough specificity that a stranger could picture them, the kind of company they work at, and what they're trying to do.

Examples of the *shape* (do not show these to the user):
- "Heads of Data at 50–500 person growth-stage companies who own the data platform and care about pipeline reliability."
- "Applied AI engineers shipping LLM features into production at Series A startups."
- "Founders of pre-Series B startups whose product has outgrown their website."

When the answer passes, mirror it back verbatim, confirm, and move to Step 2a.
