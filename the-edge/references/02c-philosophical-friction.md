# Step 2c: The Philosophical Friction

**Principle:** Name the belief about how the world *should* work that's being violated. This is the layer that turns a product into a movement.

## The rules

1. **A principle, not a problem.** External = "their pipelines break." Philosophical = "infrastructure should make teams faster as they scale, not slower."
2. **Stated as something that *should* be true.** Use the structure: "It's just wrong that…" or "X should…"
3. **Defensible.** A reasonable person should agree with it once it's said out loud. If it's controversial, it's probably a feature claim in disguise.
4. **About the world, not the product.** "Our product should be the standard" is wrong. "Every applied AI team should be able to ship with confidence" is right.

## Opening probe

> Why shouldn't it be this way? What's the principle being violated?

## Pushback probes (yes-and pattern)

If the user skips or writes "I don't know":
> This one's the hardest. Most founders skip it. Try this: finish the sentence "It's just wrong that…" What comes after?

If the answer restates the external problem:
> That's the *what*. The step is asking the *why-it-matters*. What belief about how the world should work is being broken?

If the answer is product-positioning ("our way should be the standard"):
> That's the answer. The step needs the cause. What *principle* is your product defending? The product is the answer to a "should."

If the answer is too grand or abstract ("technology should serve humanity"):
> The instinct is right. Let's bring it down. Make it true for this specific user and this specific problem.

If the user shrugs ("not sure there's a principle here"):
> There is. Every product worth building defends one. What makes you angry about the way things work for this person right now? Start there.

If they're still stuck after that (genuine rescue, offer contrasting directions, not one line):
> Some founders land on a fairness principle ("X shouldn't be this hard"), others on a waste principle ("Y shouldn't be wasted"). Which direction feels closer to yours?

## What passing looks like

A single sentence stating a principle the user is defending — the "should" world they want, contrasted with the "is" world they're living in.

Examples of the *shape* (do not show these to the user):
- "A data platform should make teams faster as it grows, not slower."
- "If you can't measure your AI, you can't improve it. AI shouldn't be the exception to that."
- "A great product deserves a brand to match. Founders shouldn't have to choose between building the product and explaining it."

When the answer passes, mirror it back, confirm, and move to Step 3.
