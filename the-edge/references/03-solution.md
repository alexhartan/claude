# Step 3: The Solution

**Principle:** Show the user that you understand their problem (empathy) and have the right to fix it (authority). Both halves are required.

## The rules

1. **Two halves, both filled.** Empathy alone reads as sympathy. Authority alone reads as bragging. Together they read as "this is the person I want in the room."
2. **Empathy is specific, not generic.** "We get it" is wrong. "We've spent four years inside this exact problem" is right.
3. **Authority is earned, not claimed.** Credentials, lived experience, track record, original work. Not adjectives.
4. **No feature lists.** Features come later. This step is about *standing*. Why the user should trust *you* to fix it.

## Opening probe

> How does your product meet them in that friction? Two halves: what you understand about their problem, and what gives you the right to solve it.

## Sub-prompts (offer if the user is stuck)

- What do you understand about their problem that competitors don't?
- What gives you the authority to solve it?

## Pushback probes (yes-and pattern)

If the answer is a feature list ("we have X, Y, Z"):
> The features are clear. The step is about standing. What makes *you* the right ones to fix this, not someone else?

If only one half is filled (empathy without authority):
> You've shown you get the problem. Now the other half. What gives you the credibility to fix it? Track record, origin story, original research, something real.

If only one half is filled (authority without empathy):
> You've shown the credentials. Now the other half. What do you understand about this problem that someone with the same credentials but no skin in the game wouldn't?

If the empathy half is generic ("we understand the pain"):
> "We understand" is the line every competitor uses. What's the specific *thing* you understand that they don't?

If the authority half is generic ("we have years of experience"):
> Experience is the right zone. Let's get specific. Who built what, when? Make it impossible to copy.

If the answer is the founder's bio:
> The bio is part of it. The other part: what's the *product's* authority? Why does this thing, built by these people, deserve trust?

## What passing looks like

A two-part answer: one half showing specific empathy, one half showing concrete authority. Together, they make the user think "yes, I want these people in my corner."

Examples of the *shape* (do not show these to the user):
- *Empathy:* "We've sat where you sit. We ran the data platform at [company] before we built this."
- *Authority:* "Built by the original creators of [open-source project that's already in your stack]."

When the answer passes, mirror it back, confirm, and move to Step 4.
