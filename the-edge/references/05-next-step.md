# Step 5: The Next Step

**Principle:** Two CTAs — one for people ready to commit, one for people not ready yet. Both required.

## The rules

1. **Two and only two.** A direct ask, and a transitional ask. Not three. Not "explore" or "learn more."
2. **Direct = commitment.** Book a demo, start a trial, get started, talk to sales. Something that begins a buying conversation.
3. **Transitional = value without commitment.** A free resource, tool, benchmark, calculator, audit, guide, playbook. Something that's useful even if they never buy.
4. **Transitional is high-value, not low-effort.** "Subscribe to newsletter" is not transitional value. The transitional CTA earns trust by giving away expertise.

## Opening probe

> What's the single clearest thing you want them to do right now? You'll need two: the direct ask, and a transitional ask for people not ready yet.

## Pushback probes (yes-and pattern)

If both CTAs are direct ("book demo" + "schedule a call"):
> Two strong direct asks. The step needs one of each. The transitional is for people not ready to talk yet. Something free they can do alone. A guide, a tool, a benchmark.

If the transitional CTA is weak ("subscribe to newsletter," "follow us on LinkedIn"):
> Newsletter is the easy choice. The step needs something with more give. What's something that would help them even if they never bought from you?

If the direct CTA is vague ("learn more," "explore"):
> "Learn more" is the polite version. The step needs the actual commitment. Demo, trial, intro call?

If the user gives three CTAs:
> All three are real. The step needs two. Three CTAs is the same as no CTA. Which transitional ask is the strongest?

If the transitional CTA is gated ("download our 60-page whitepaper after a 12-field form"):
> The asset is strong. The wrapper is heavy. If the form takes longer than the gift, it's not a gift. Lighten the ask.

## What passing looks like

Two CTAs:
- **Direct:** A concrete next step toward becoming a customer.
- **Transitional:** A free, high-value resource a stranger would actually use.

Examples of the *shape* (do not show these to the user):
- Direct: "Book a 20-min Sharpening Session." Transitional: "Run The Edge on your own positioning."
- Direct: "Start a free trial." Transitional: "Download the LLM Evaluation Playbook."
- Direct: "Book a technical demo." Transitional: "Read the benchmark report."

When the answer passes, mirror both back, confirm, and move to Step 6.
